## The race at a glance

- **Election day:** Mon Oct 26, 10 a.m.–8 p.m. Advance voting already ran Oct 6–11. [City of Toronto](https://www.toronto.ca/news/toronto-city-clerk-certifies-361-candidates-for-2026-municipal-election/)
- **Candidates:** 53 certified for mayor. Chris Alexander ended his campaign on Oct 6 but **remains on the ballot**, because the withdrawal deadline had passed. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Toronto_mayoral_election), [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/toronto/advance-voting-toronto-municipal-election-9.7377450)

- **Polls:** early-October polls show Chow ahead of Bradford by 3–9 points (Mainstreet, Scope, Forum, Liaison, Nanos). [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Toronto_mayoral_election)
- **Remaining debate:** Oct 15, hosted by Rotman and TVO, moderated by Steve Paikin.

## Olivia Chow (incumbent)

**Past offices**
- Toronto Board of Education trustee, 1985–91.
- Metro Toronto councillor, 1992–97.
- Toronto city councillor, 1998–2005.
- NDP MP for Trinity–Spadina, 2006–14.
- Mayor since July 12, 2023.

**Past mayoral runs**
- 2014: finished 3rd with 23.15%.
- 2023 by-election: won with 37.17% (269,372 votes).

**Record as mayor** (Wikipedia unless noted; check each item against City records)
- **Property tax increases:** 9.5% (2024), 6.9% (2025, including a 1.5% city building levy), 2.2% (2026). The 2.2% figure is already a verified fact on our site.
- **TTC fares:** frozen each year of her term.
- **New Deal with Ontario (Nov 2023):** the Province takes over the Gardiner and DVP; $1.2B in operating funding and $7.6B in capital relief over three years.
- **Housing:** a target of 65,000 rent-controlled homes by 2030. Rival Bradford says about 1,500 have been built; Chow's campaign disputes his framing. [CP24](https://www.cp24.com/gta-elections-2026/2026/10/01/chow-vows-to-not-slash-city-services-as-bradford-targets-torontos-budget-hole/)
- **Credit rating:** the City reports an upgrade to AA+, which the campaign describes as the first upgrade in 23 years. [City of Toronto](https://www.toronto.ca/news/city-of-toronto-launches-2026-budget/)
- **2026 budget:** shelter $912M → $786M, police $1.43B net, TTC +$93.8M, and $1.9B of capital work deferred.

**2026 promises** (from news coverage; the campaign's own platform page is still to be found)
- **TTC:** free rides for the rest of the week after 10 paid trips, starting in 2027, plus a fourth year of frozen fares. [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/toronto/article/olivia-chow-wants-to-make-the-ttc-free-after-10-paid-rides-a-week/)
- **Housing:** shovels in the ground on 10,000 rental homes in 2027, toward the 65,000 target. [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/toronto/article/olivia-chow-promises-to-build-10000-new-rental-homes-next-year/)
- **Property tax:** increases kept "at or around" inflation.
- **Seniors:** a $5.61M plan covering homemaking help for 5,000 seniors, a 4th mobile dental clinic, and 2 primary care vans. [NOW](https://nowtoronto.com/news/toronto-election-2026-chow-senior-plan-bradford-land-transfer-tax-cut-campaign-promises/)
- **Groceries:** grocery support for families with school-age children, plus action on price gouging.

## Brad Bradford

**Past offices:** Ward 19 Beaches–East York councillor since 2018 (re-elected in 2022 with 54.71%).
- Chair of the Planning and Housing Committee after the 2022 election, then vice-chair under Chow.
- Removed as vice-chair in Dec 2024. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Bradford)

**Past mayoral runs:** 2023 by-election, finished 8th with 1.28% (9,254 votes).

**Record on council** (Wikipedia; votes should be pulled from our own council-votes dataset)
- 2020: supported the unsuccessful motion to cut the police budget by 10%.
- 2023: supported the cycling expansion that included the Bloor bike lanes to Resurrection Rd. He now says council "got this one wrong."
- June 2024: his motion to block the Sankofa Square renaming and redirect the money did not pass.
- 2024: the Integrity Commissioner found he improperly used constituent contact data for campaign emails in 2023; he apologized. *Primary source to cite: the Integrity Commissioner's report.*

**2026 promises** (from news coverage; the campaign's own platform page is still to be found)
- **Property tax:** frozen in year one, then increases below inflation, plus an independent line-by-line spending review. [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/toronto/article/torontos-mayoral-election-is-four-weeks-away-heres-everything-you-need-to-know-if-you-havent-been-paying-attention/)
- **Land transfer tax:** eliminate the municipal tax on the first $1.1M for first-time buyers and in-city movers. His campaign estimates the cost at about $300M a year. [NOW](https://nowtoronto.com/news/toronto-election-2026-chow-senior-plan-bradford-land-transfer-tax-cut-campaign-promises/)
- **Capital funding:** stop setting aside operating money for future capital projects and borrow more instead. He said his plan "means that the city will borrow more than it does today." [CP24](https://www.cp24.com/gta-elections-2026/2026/10/01/chow-vows-to-not-slash-city-services-as-bradford-targets-torontos-budget-hole/)
- **Police:** 1,000 net new officers in his first term.
- **Water:** a publicly owned Toronto Water utility and lower water bills. Reports conflict between about $300 a year and 25%, so check the campaign's own materials.
- **Other:** remove the Bloor bike lanes west of Runnymede; rename Sankofa Square "Toronto Square" and add a police sub-station there.
- Chow's campaign says Bradford's promises total $6B and calls the plan "reckless".

## Chris Alexander (campaign ended Oct 6; still on the ballot)

- **Past offices:** Conservative MP for Ajax–Pickering, 2011–15; Minister of Citizenship and Immigration, 2013–15; ambassador to Afghanistan, 2003–05.
- **Past mayoral runs:** none.

## What Toronto needs: two kinds of evidence (we report both, we don't pick)

**1. What the City's own documents say it is short on**
- **Budget gap:** City Manager Paul Johnson warned in Jan 2025 of a shortfall of about $700M as early as 2027. [CP24](https://cp24.com/politics/toronto-city-hall/2025/01/13/toronto-staff-propose-69-per-cent-tax-bump-including-city-building-levy) Budget chief Carroll describes a structural gap of about $1B since amalgamation.
- **Long-Term Financial Plan (2023):** a $46.5B operating and capital shortfall over 10 years. [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6938094)
- **Infrastructure:** about $26B is needed over 10 years to keep services in repair (reported May 2024). [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/toronto/article/falling-apart-toronto-facing-26b-infrastructure-shortfall-to-maintain-service-over-next-decade/) *To do: find the City report this comes from.*
- **2026 budget:** $788M in "efficiencies and offsets", $1.23B in New Deal support, and $1.9B of capital work deferred. [City of Toronto](https://www.toronto.ca/news/city-of-toronto-launches-2026-budget/)

**2. What residents say** (polls; results depend on the wording)
- **Nanos for CP24/CTV, Sept 30–Oct 4, n=755, ±3.6:** asked for the "single biggest problem", residents named traffic 26%, housing costs 18%, public safety 16%, property taxes 12% and groceries 9%. **Gen Z and younger millennials put housing costs first, at 31%.** In Scarborough, 57% want tax increases kept low even if that means fewer services. [CP24](https://www.cp24.com/gta-elections-2026/2026/10/08/what-is-the-single-biggest-problem-in-toronto-new-exclusive-polling-reveals-whats-on-voters-minds/)
- **Toronto Star/Ipsos, Sept 2026:** cost of living is the top concern, up 5 points from the year before. [Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2026-09/Toronto%20Star%202026%20Ipsos%20Poll_Full_Report.pdf)
- **Mainstreet, June 2026:** traffic 27.3%, affordability 25.8%, crime 23.5%.

**Note for the guide:** "Getting around" in our rooms covers traffic as well as the TTC. Traffic is the top issue in two polls, but our transit room only shows the TTC's cost, so we may want to add road and congestion spending to that room.
